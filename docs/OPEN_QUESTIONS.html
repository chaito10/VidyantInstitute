Vidyarthi — Open Questions and Decision Log

Updated: 22 October 2026
Purpose: Record the user’s current decisions and keep unresolved contradictions visible. The answers below are not silently converted into technical or legal guarantees.

Confirmed current direction

Q1. Flat participation and “no authority”

Decision given: “Go with Flat participants no authority at all.”

Still unresolved - Does “no authority” mean no permanent rank, or does it also rule out any temporary issuer, assessor, moderator, or grievance-resolution capability? - If no participant can authorize or validate anything, how are government/industry attestations and wallet uniqueness represented? - Who can respond to fraud, malicious course packages, abuse, or a grievance without creating a governance or authority mechanism?

Do not implement: Do not replace static roles with an invented capability-granting policy. The current target rejects permanent hierarchy, but the exact authorization model is unresolved.

Q2. First MVP and course format

Decision given: Start with the first AI-generated MOOC, built from micro-learning content with a consistent format throughout; course requirements/design are peer-designed.

Still unresolved - What subject and learner level should the first MOOC target? - What exact fields are mandatory in every micro-learning module? - Which AI-generated artifacts are accepted automatically, and what automated quality gates must pass before publication? - What is the end-to-end MVP acceptance test on phone and laptop?

Q3. Employer-facing credentials

Decision given: Use real projects, assignments, and validation from government and industries.

Still unresolved - Which government bodies or industry partners, if any, are actual participants? None are assumed to be committed. - What counts as validation: endorsement, an assessment rubric, a signed attestation, or hiring outcomes? - How will the system distinguish community-issued, employer-endorsed, government-validated, accredited, and legally recognized credentials?

Q4. Naming

Decision given: Name the product Vidyarthi.

Decision updated: Vidyarthi is now the canonical working product name. VidyantInstitute remains the repository name for now; SageEduMint and OpenDAO University are historical/working names.

Still unresolved: Should the repository name eventually be changed? This is not a blocker for the MVP.

Q5. Pathways and credits

Decision given: Credits should be on an open blockchain and verifiable by anyone; learners have multiple entry/exit points independent of prior certification; accumulated credits may be formalized into programs.

Still unresolved - Which blockchain or credential/record format is intended? - Are “credits” learning-effort units, competency measures, or formal academic credits? These are not interchangeable. - Who defines program thresholds and equivalencies if the platform has no governance or authorization model? - How can open verification avoid publishing personal or sensitive learning records?

Q6. Curriculum governance and peer review

Decision given: Individuals may publish without required authorization or verification. Peer review of papers/theses is optional and may use open comments; AI evaluation is sufficient as the default.

Still unresolved - What automated validation is mandatory for course packages, assessments, generated references, and executable content? - Can an author opt out of peer review while still making a credential claim based on the work? - How are corrections, plagiarism allegations, and unsafe or malicious material handled without a governance mechanism?

Q7. Credits and compiled results

Decision given: Generate descriptive and quantitative measures that can be compiled through a program into a final result for job scrutiny or other purposes.

Still unresolved - Define each quantitative measure, its units, weighting, and reproducibility. - Who chooses program compilation rules, and can employers interpret them consistently? - How are changed rubrics or reassessed work reflected in historical results?

Q8. Assessment

Decision given: All assessment should be automated.

Still unresolved - Which assessment types can be reliably automated, and what happens when the model cannot grade confidently? - What prevents generated tests from being fabricated, self-serving, or easy to game? - How are false positives, accessibility needs, and appeals handled?

Q9. Human intervention and grievances

Decision given: No human intervention is required until a grievance is raised.

Still unresolved - Who receives and decides grievances if the system has no governance or authority model? - What evidence, response times, appeal stages, and temporary safeguards apply? - Can a disputed result be marked as contested without silently deleting the original evidence?

Q10. Evidence and retention

Decision given: “It’s blockchain, human life time.”

Interpretation not yet confirmed: This may mean blockchain-backed evidence retained for the person’s lifetime.

Still unresolved - Which data is on-chain, and which stays local or encrypted/off-chain? - Does “lifetime” mean the learner’s lifetime, the credential’s lifetime, or permanent retention? - How do key loss, correction, revocation, privacy, and deletion requests work with immutable records?

Q11. Wallet identity

Decision given: A wallet, one unique wallet per person.

Still unresolved - How is one-person-one-wallet established without collecting excessive identity data or introducing a central authority? - Are pseudonymous wallets permitted? How are wallet recovery, key rotation, compromise, and succession handled? - What evidence, if any, links a wallet to a legal person for employer/government validation?

Q12. Web3 security

Decision given: Use Web3 standards for security.

Still unresolved - Select the concrete wallet, signature, key-management, credential, and ZK proof standards. - Define a threat model covering replay, malicious modules, key theft, Sybil identities, privacy leakage, and compromised dependencies. - Which operations must be local-verifiable, and what happens when revocation information is unavailable offline?

Q13. Local records and ZK verification

Decision given: All records are local; verification uses ZK apps.

Blocking tension: Q5 calls for open-blockchain credits verifiable by anyone, while Q10 suggests lifetime blockchain evidence. These can conflict with local-only records and privacy.

Still unresolved - Does “all records local” mean raw personal records remain local while users selectively publish proofs/commitments? - Which facts should a ZK proof reveal, and which must remain private? - How are public verification, revocation, correction, and data minimization handled?

Q14. Peer network

Decision given: Fossil SCM + libp2p is preferred.

Still unresolved - Which data is synchronized through Fossil and which messages/services use libp2p? - What are the MVP offline and sync-conflict acceptance tests? - How are peers discovered and spam/malicious peers handled without a central authority?

Q15. Governance

Decision given: “No governance model.”

Blocking tension: The project also expects grievances, employer/government validation, open credentials, automated assessment, and correction/revocation of evidence. These require defined procedures even if the project rejects platform-wide governance.

Still unresolved - Does “no governance” mean no voting/DAO, no central administrators, or no shared policy at all? - Can protocol maintainers publish software updates without controlling learner records or credentials? - What minimal process, if any, exists for security incidents, grievances, and incompatible protocol versions?

Q16. MVP acceptance criteria

Decision given: The complete course should work on a phone or laptop.

Still unresolved - Which phone/browser and laptop/browser combinations are supported first? - Must the course work fully offline after initial download? - What is the minimum complete module: content, quiz, practical assignment, automated result, local evidence, and export? - What resource limits and automated test suite define “works”?

Q17. Warranty and liability

Decision given: No warranty or liability.

Still unresolved - Draft appropriate terms and disclaimers with qualified legal review for the jurisdictions where the product is offered. - Which legal obligations or liabilities cannot be excluded by contract? - How are third-party AI providers, user-generated content, security issues, and credential misrepresentation addressed?

Caution: A “no warranty/liability” statement does not itself guarantee that all liability can legally be excluded.

Q18. Costs and sustainability

Decision given: Costs are borne by peers or students.

Still unresolved - Which costs are paid by students: AI inference, storage, blockchain fees, network bandwidth, hosting, or all of them? - Must learning, local verification, and basic course access remain possible at zero monetary cost? - Can BYOK/local AI avoid mandatory provider spending, and how are costs disclosed before a task runs?

Q19. Standards and stack

Decision given: Web3, wallets, ZK apps, Fossil, and Rust.

Still unresolved - Select specific interoperable standards rather than treating these technology categories as standards themselves. - Define stable module, assessment, evidence, wallet, and credential interfaces. - Which components are required for the first release, and which remain optional adapters?

Q20. Employer search portal

Decision given: Build a portal that lets employers submit search queries.

Still unresolved - What can employers search: public portfolio data, learner-approved evidence, credentials, or private records? - Does a learner approve disclosure for each query or profile? - How are ranking bias, stale evidence, and misleading credential labels avoided?

Q21. Blockchain overhead

Decision given: Zero overhead; if costs are borne, they should be borne by the student.

Still unresolved - Does “zero overhead” mean zero platform fee, zero gas fee, zero extra infrastructure, or negligible user effort? - Which chain or layer is intended, and how will fees be measured on ordinary phones? - Should blockchain be in the MVP, or should the MVP prove the learning loop before adding it?

Q22. PhD-equivalent work

Decision given: “If work is equivalent to PhD it is a PhD.”

Important distinction: The platform can assess or document PhD-level work/competency, but that alone does not make it a formally awarded or legally recognized PhD degree. A formal degree claim depends on applicable law and a recognized awarding authority.

Still unresolved - Should the platform issue a “PhD-level competency/research portfolio” record, while formal-degree status is shown separately? - What standards and independent evaluation determine equivalence? - Which recognized institution or legally authorized body, if any, can confer the formal degree?

Q23. Protocol and tooling

Decision given: The protocol and tools should let anyone run a complete module; keep the design minimal, abstract, modular, and BYOK for AI.

Still unresolved - Define the module contract and the smallest reference runner. - Specify deterministic inputs/outputs, assessment interfaces, evidence export, and compatibility/versioning. - Define what “anyone can run” means for offline use, supported hardware, dependencies, and AI provider configuration.
